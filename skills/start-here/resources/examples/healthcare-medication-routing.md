# Example: routing a hospital process improvement lead's open-ended question to root-cause

**User:** What can you do for lean work?

**Assistant:** This toolkit has eight working tools: a3-coach structures a problem onto one page for review; root-cause runs a structured 5-Whys or fishbone on something that keeps happening; kaizen-charter plans a focused improvement event; standard-work and twi-jbs document how a job is done today, or build the trainer's version of it; vsm-calc turns walk observations into value-stream numbers; oee-from-csv turns a scheduling or downtime export into equipment or room utilization (an MRI, an OR, an analyzer); gemba-walk structures a walk of the unit. It's here so the paperwork gets out of the way and you can be on the unit instead of at a keyboard.

What's in front of you right now?

**User:** We've had a few medication near-misses on one of our units and the charge nurse wants a root cause done before the safety committee meets.

**Assistant:** That's a recurring safety issue you need the real cause for — that's `root-cause`. It'll scope it with an IS/IS-NOT table, pick 5 Whys or fishbone depending on how many factors are involved, and it won't let "the clinician didn't check" stand as the final answer — it'll push past that to something systemic and fixable. Before you share any notes or the incident report there: leave out names of patients or clinicians, and anything the organization treats as confidential — roles and initials are fine. And please don't paste any protected health information — no patient identifiers, no chart numbers, no dates of birth tied to a name.

**User:** The committee is also going to ask whether we need to write anyone up over this. How should I handle that?

**Assistant:** That's worth naming plainly rather than dodging: root-cause won't accept "the clinician didn't check" as a final answer, because it almost never explains why the near-miss happened on its own — it'll push to what made the error possible in the first place. Whether anyone is written up is a personnel decision outside what this toolkit does; what it can give the committee is a systemic cause worth fixing, which is usually the more useful thing to bring to that meeting.

That's root-cause from here — tell it what you've seen and it'll take it from there.

---

**What this skill did:**
- Answered "what can you do for lean work" in the hospital setting's own terms, asked the one routing question, and routed the medication near-miss to `root-cause` based on "keeps happening" and "before the safety committee."
- Gave the healthcare-specific data-safety line, including the protected-health-information sentence, before any paste.

**What still needs a human:**
- root-cause will need the actual near-miss details, frequency, and unit — not yet captured here.
- Whether this is a single incident or a genuine pattern is unconfirmed; root-cause will scope that with IS/IS-NOT.
