## When to use / when not to use

**Use** when preparing for or debriefing after a floor observation — pre-walk planning (what to look for, questions to ask), real-time note capture during the walk, or structured debrief (what was seen, what was learned, what happens next). Use to coach leaders new to the discipline.

**Don't use** as a substitute for a full `5s-audit` (scored, zone-by-zone), a time study, or a formal `standard-work` document. A gemba walk is a learning activity — it surfaces questions, not audit scores.

---

## The discipline: observe, don't solve

The single most important rule: **gemba is a learning discipline, not an inspection.** Leaders who walk the floor to find problems to fix — and announce fixes on the spot — destroy the discipline. Workers learn to hide problems or present only polished conditions. The walk becomes theater.

The correct posture: go to understand. Ask questions. Thank people for what you observe, good and bad. Leave with learning, not with a correction list you hand to the supervisor.

## Five observation lenses

Walk each zone with all five lenses active. Do not organize the walk by lens — organize it by process flow, then apply all five as you go.

### 1. Safety
What could injure someone? Look for: pinch points, slip/trip hazards, blocked emergency exits, missing machine guards, workers in awkward postures, materials stored above shoulder height. Do not walk past a live safety hazard without acting on it — this is the one exception to "observe, don't solve." Safety findings require immediate containment.

### 2. Quality
Where are defects created? Where are they caught? Look for: rework piles, inspection stations that have become informal rework stations, operators sorting parts, scrap containers that are full or overflowing, recent changes to the process that have not been reflected in standard work.

### 3. Flow
Where does work stop? Look for: queue between operations, waiting operators, batching behavior, materials on the floor that are not in a designated location, operations running ahead and creating WIP downstream, operations running behind and starving downstream stations.

### 4. Standard Work
Is the work being done the way the standard says? Look for: operators not following the posted sequence, tools not in their designated location, standard work documents that are outdated, laminated sheets that have been modified by hand, operators who cannot find or have not seen the standard.

Cross-reference `standard-work` if the standard documents themselves are missing or inadequate.

### 5. Engagement
Do people understand how they are performing and why it matters? Look for: team boards that are not updated, metrics no one references, operators who do not know the status of their line, supervisors who deliver information rather than ask questions, problems reported but not acted on.

## Ask / don't-ask rules

**Open questions — use freely:**
- "Can you show me how you do that step?"
- "What makes this step difficult?"
- "What happens when [X condition] occurs?"
- "How do you know when the process is running well?"
- "What would make this easier?"

**Closed or leading questions — avoid:**
- "Why aren't you following the standard?" — accusatory
- "Isn't that a safety hazard?" — leading
- "Shouldn't this be cleaned up?" — inspection mindset
- "Did you know you're supposed to…?" — teaching in the moment, not learning

**The interrogation trap:** a string of rapid questions, especially to a single operator, turns a walk into an audit. Ask one question, listen fully, follow the operator's answer with curiosity rather than correction.

## Structured debrief

After every walk, run a debrief before the observations are lost. Do not skip this step. Debrief should happen within 30 minutes of returning.

**Debrief structure:**
1. What did you observe? (facts only — no interpretation yet)
2. What surprised you?
3. What did you learn that you didn't know before the walk?
4. What questions does this raise that need more data or investigation?
5. What, if anything, requires immediate action? (Safety first)
6. What goes to the T1/T2 board as an open item? Reference `daily-management` for tier routing.

Never leave the debrief with a list of corrections for the supervisor without also capturing what you learned. A walk that only produces a punch list is an inspection, not a gemba walk.

---

## Quality bar

1. Pre-walk preparation identifies specific questions in at least three of the five lenses.
2. Observation notes distinguish facts from interpretations — "operator stepped around pallet" not "operator was being unsafe."
3. No on-the-spot solutions delivered to workers during the walk, except for live safety hazards.
4. Debrief captures at least one learning (something the observer did not know before) in addition to problems.
5. Every immediate action item from the debrief has an owner and a due date.
6. Open items are routed to the appropriate daily management tier board.
7. The walk route follows process flow sequence, not organizational structure.
