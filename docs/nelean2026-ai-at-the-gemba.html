A question for the room

Last week, how many hours did you spend at a keyboard producing documents about the gemba?

  • A3Drafting, reformatting, re-drafting for the steering committee
  • OEEWrestling a downtime export into a spreadsheet

Where those hours come from

Every one of them is an hour not at the gemba. The document is necessary. The keyboard time is not value-added — it's the transportation and motion waste of the CI function itself.

Design principle 1 of 5

Jidoka for information.

Every number carries a source tag. An untagged number is a defect passed downstream — so there aren't any.

Background — Line 3 changeover

Changeover, current:  47 min  [observed, 6 runs]
Target (prod. plan):  20 min  [from system: MES]
Capacity freed:       2.1 hr/day [user estimate]
Die-cart travel:      [NEEDS GEMBA]
Who does ext. setup:  [NEEDS GEMBA]

observed  you saw it, counted it, timed it

from system  MES, historian, maintenance log

user estimate  a guess, labeled as one

[NEEDS GEMBA]  the andon. Go look.

No tag? Don't trust it. Ask where it came from. That's the whole discipline.

AI at the Gemba07

13:00 · ~3 min

Jidoka: build quality in, stop rather than pass a defect. The biggest real danger of these tools is a confident-sounding number with no provenance. So the rule is: every figure says where it came from, and if it came from nowhere, the assistant says so — in yellow.

Make the andon connection explicit. "[NEEDS GEMBA] is the cord. The assistant pulled it. Now you go."

Design principle 2 of 5

Poka-yoke for data.

The classic traps are designed out. The tool won't let you fall into them silently.

Missing column

It lists what it found and what it still needs. It never substitutes a value.

Reason code it can't map

Goes in an explicit Unmapped row with the raw text — never force-fit into "minor stops." A big Unmapped bucket is itself a finding.

OEE over 100%

Flagged, not printed. Almost always means "ideal cycle time" is an average, not best-demonstrated — and the assistant asks.

Planned vs. unplanned downtime not separated? It says so. Changeover hiding inside run time as a Performance loss? It says so. These are the mistakes that misdirect a countermeasure — the tool's job is to refuse to make them quietly.

AI at the Gemba08

16:00 · ~2 min

Every one of these traps is one this room has personally fallen into with a spreadsheet. The average-vs-best-demonstrated one will get knowing laughs. Use it.

The key sentence: "A spreadsheet will happily compute 104% OEE. This won't."

Design principle 3 of 5

Observe, don't solve.

The gemba-walk skill is taught the same discipline you'd teach a new plant manager. It will not hand you a punch list.

It will ask

  • "Can you show me how you do that step?"
  • "What makes this step difficult?"
  • "How do you know when it's running well?"

It won't ask

  • "Why aren't you following the standard?"
  • "Isn't that a safety hazard?"
  • "Did you know you're supposed to…?"

Debrief structure: what did you see, what surprised you, what did you learn, what needs more data — and only then, what needs action. A walk that only produces corrections is an inspection. The skill enforces that, because we wrote it in.

Design principle 4 of 5

Sensei first. Draft on demand.

The coaching skills ask a few questions before they draft — left-to-right on the A3, the way you were coached.

Which problem, for whom, what have you seen, what does the data say. It won't jump to countermeasures because you haven't earned them yet.

Say "just draft it" and it skips the questions.

You get a draft in seconds — with every fact it doesn't have marked [NEEDS GEMBA] instead of invented. A draft full of yellow tags after a first pass is normal. It's your to-do list for the floor.

Design principle 5 of 5 · emphasis on the people

Respect for people, written in.

  • 01Ask it "how many people can we cut" and it answers the process question — then says once, plainly: these tools free capacity; what the organization does with freed capacity is a leadership decision.No lecture. No refusal. But it will not do that math for you as if it were neutral.
  • 02Before anyone pastes a document: leave out names of employees or patients — roles and initials are fine.Said every time, in every setting. Not buried in a policy.
  • 03No AI jargon. Ever. "What you gave me." "I made that up — check it."It mirrors your vocabulary — PDSA if you say PDSA, unit and clinician if you're in a hospital. It never corrects you.

Every deliverable ends the same way

It always sends you back to the floor.

What this skill did

The analysis, the key finding, the one caveat that matters.

What still needs a human

The assumption that changes the answer if it's wrong. Every yellow tag. The decision it doesn't have the authority or context to make.

Suggested next step

One step. Not a list. Usually a person's name and a place on the floor.

The tool's last word is always a hand-off — to you, at the gemba.

33:00 · ~2 min

Standardized closing block across all seventeen skills. The design intent: the assistant never gets the last word on what's true. A practitioner does, standing next to the machine.

Honestly

Where this goes wrong.

  • 01The practitioner who stops going.The tool amplifies the one who goes and sees. It hollows out the one who doesn't. Same tool, opposite outcomes — and the difference is a habit, not a feature.
  • 02It sounds confident.It always will. Confidence is not a source tag. Read the tags, not the tone.
  • 03Garbage in, beautifully formatted garbage out.An average cycle time labeled "ideal" produces a flattering, wrong Performance number — faster than ever before.
  • 04Confidential data in a chat box.Know your company's policy before the first paste. The data-safety line is a reminder, not a control.

How to start · you already know this cycle

One practitioner. One skill. One real problem. Two weeks.

P

Pick the document you dread most. Pick one person who already goes to the gemba. Give them one skill.

D

Use it on a live problem, not a pilot. Hand it what you'd hand a new Black Belt.

C

One measure: did hours at the gemba go up? Not "did we use AI." Check every tagged number against its source.

A

If yes — write your own skill for your own plant's document. If no — stop. It's a clipboard, not a strategy.
